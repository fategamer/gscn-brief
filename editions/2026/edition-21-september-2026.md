### THE BIG ISSUE  
**Kenya’s e-GP–IFMIS Integration Deadline: Visibility or Just Another System?**

**Verified fact**  
On 25 August 2026, Treasury CS John Mbadi directed all 47 county governments to complete full technical integration of the e-GP platform with IFMIS by the end of September. The system, mandatory for all public procurement since 1 July 2026, is already used by more than 1,500 procuring entities. Treasury projects potential annual savings of up to KSh 85.9 billion (≈ $657 million) once the end-to-end digital chain is operational.

**Interpretation**  
Integration is the missing link. e-GP digitises the front end (planning, tendering, award). IFMIS controls the back end (budget commitment, payment). Without a clean interface, entities can still create “ghost” commitments or delay payments outside the digital trail. Parallel moves — KRA linking eTIMS invoices to IFMIS — show the government is closing the loop from tender to tax-compliant payment.

**Implication**  
For procurement professionals and suppliers, the next 90 days are decisive. Counties that finish integration will process tenders and payments faster and with fewer disputes. Those that lag risk rejected claims, delayed cash flow and heightened PPRA scrutiny. Private-sector suppliers should treat e-GP registration and clean digital invoicing as non-negotiable operating requirements, not optional compliance.

**>4.3 million TEU** of containership capacity currently waiting to berth globally — higher than the previous all-time peak recorded during the height of the COVID congestion crisis.

What this number means for procurement professionals: Vessel supply is no longer the main bottleneck. Port and inland capacity are. Expect longer lead times, higher demurrage exposure, and greater need for multi-port or multi-mode contingency planning through Q4 2026.

### THE PROCUREMENT GAP
**The system went live. The data didn’t.**

Many organisations have migrated to e-GP or upgraded ERP modules, yet still treat supplier master data, contract metadata and performance KPIs as afterthoughts. Result: digital processes run on incomplete or outdated information, producing the same delays and disputes the technology was meant to eliminate.

---

### EXECUTION WINS
**Treat every new digital requirement as a supplier-enablement project, not just an internal compliance task.**

When e-GP, eTIMS or EPR automation goes live, the organisations that win are those that proactively train and support their key suppliers — not those that simply issue a circular and wait for problems to appear.
